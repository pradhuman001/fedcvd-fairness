
\section{A trained MLP (exploratory)}
\label{sec:mlp}
The MLP (16 tanh units) uses $T{=}400$ rounds at rate 0.1 and reaches the same unpenalized AUROC as logistic regression (0.797 natural, 0.795 capped). Penalized MLPs are the least reliable models in this study. In a single-seed check (capped, seed 42, $\lambda{=}8$) the intersectional DP gap was 0.091, 0.125 and 0.135 after 50, 150 and 400 rounds at rate 0.1--0.25, and EO$_\text{int}$ 0.074, 0.116 and 0.133, so the earlier $T{=}50$ MLP overstated what the penalty achieves; at $T{=}400$ a rate of 0.25 instead of 0.1 still gave EO$_\text{int}$ 0.208 against 0.133 at nearly identical AUROC (0.686 vs.\ 0.687). We therefore treat all MLP results as exploratory and do not draw conclusions from small differences.

With that caveat (Tables~\ref{tab:mlp}, \ref{tab:mlpoff}): the sex-only MLP shows the same masking pattern as logistic regression (natural: sex DP $0.125\to0.045$, $-64\%$, but DP$_\text{int}$ $0.266\to0.196$, $-26\%$; capped DP$_\text{int}$ $0.260\to0.190$, $-27\%$). The intersectional penalty is \emph{less} effective on DP for the MLP than for logistic regression (DP$_\text{int}$ 0.087 natural, 0.096 capped at $\lambda{=}8$, against 0.033 and 0.048), but its EO gap is lower (0.164 and 0.157). Oversampling lowers DP$_\text{int}$ more than for logistic regression ($-0.078$ [$-0.102,-0.045$] natural, 5 seeds; $-0.039$ [$-0.061,-0.018$] capped, 10 seeds). Its EO effect is positive in natural data ($+0.048$ [$-0.005,+0.096$]; label balancing $+0.056$ [$+0.021,+0.098$]) and null in capped data ($+0.003$ [$-0.054,+0.047$]). The MLP tests are exploratory and not part of the Holm family: natural has 5 seeds, for which the smallest attainable $p$ is 0.0625, so no natural MLP test can be significant at 0.05, and capped intervals are unadjusted. Group re-weighting lowers EO in natural data ($-0.013$ [$-0.020,-0.008$]) and not detectably in capped data. The MLP therefore neither confirms nor contradicts the logistic-regression EO effect of oversampling.
